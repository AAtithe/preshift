# The labour programme

Twelve weeks, run by a Williams, Stanley & Co. lead with the site's owner and GM. Software
alone does not change how a GM rosters. A programme with a baseline from the ledger, a weekly
rhythm and a result signed off in the management accounts does. This is the method. The product
supports each step, and the Programme tab tracks every site on it.

## Why we run it this way

Most workforce software promises transformation and is judged on features. Operators do not buy
features. They buy the confidence that their managers will change behaviour, and the proof that
the change reached the P&L. We are the only party in the market that can give both:

- we already hold the client's ledger and payroll, so the baseline is the true figure, not an estimate;
- we are already in the client every month for the close, so the programme rides a rhythm that exists;
- we sign the result off in the management accounts, so the saving is a number the owner trusts.

Lead with that. A scheduling vendor can say labour fell. We can show it in the accounts we produce.

## The four phases

| Weeks | Phase | What the site must do | How it is proved |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 to 2 | Baseline | Measure true labour: labour % by site and daypart, clocked time against the rota, hours rostered over demand. Load eight weeks of hourly till sales. Agree targets with the owner and GM. | Baseline recorded from the management accounts, or from till and approved time over whole weeks. Kick-off signed off by the lead. |
| 3 to 6 | Control | Build every week from the forecast. Publish at least seven days ahead with nothing blocking. Review variance with the GM every week. | Ladder rung 1 held three weeks in four. Weekly review signed off. |
| 7 to 10 | Optimise | Hit target within one point. Tighten shift edges by daypart. Take the weekly moves. Agree cross-site cover with the people willing to do it. | Rungs 2 and 3 held. Half or more of the moves taken. Cover agreement signed off. |
| 11 to 12 | Embed | Own the forecast. Close labour by working day 3. Sign the saving off in the accounts. Hand over to business as usual. | Rung 4 held. Clocked time within 1% of the rota. Day-3 close signed off. Saving verified. |

## The ladder

The ladder is how the GM sees their own progress. Each rung is a habit that must hold three
weeks in four before the next one counts, and a site needs three scored weeks before any rung
can hold. Rungs are cumulative: a site that hits target but publishes late is on rung zero,
because a late rota is the first habit to fix.

1. Rota on time and legal. Published seven or more days before the week, nothing blocking.
2. Labour on target. Within one point of target, on actual labour once time is approved.
3. Daypart precision. Five per cent or less of rostered hours over demand.
4. Owns the forecast. Hourly forecast error at or under 12%, and the GM's overrides adding value, not removing it.

The scorecard always states the next step in one sentence. Use it in the weekly review: one
habit at a time, never the whole list.

## The weekly rhythm

Thirty minutes, same day each week, the GM and the lead.

1. Last week, from the scorecard. Notice given, labour against target, hours over demand,
   clocked time against the rota, forecast error. One number at a time, and ask the GM to explain it
   before you do.
2. The moves. What was offered, what was taken, what it actually saved against the clock.
   Where a move was turned down, read the GM's reason together. "Function booked" is a
   forecast problem, not a rota problem. "Staff will not accept it" is a people problem.
   Both are worth more than the move.
3. The forecast. Where the error sat by daypart, and whether the GM's overrides beat the
   model. A GM who overrides often and loses learns to trust the model. A GM who overrides
   rarely and wins should be asked what they know that the model does not, and that becomes
   an adjustment next time.
4. This week. Run the model, add the known one-offs, build, take the moves, publish on time.

## Coaching notes for leads

- Ask before you tell. "Why do you think Sunday ran 9% under forecast?" builds a GM who
  thinks. "Sunday ran 9% under" builds a GM who waits for you.
- Praise the habit, not the number. A week on target by luck teaches nothing; a week published
  ten days ahead is a habit forming.
- Keep the saving honest. The Programme tab shows an estimate from the rota until a period is
  signed off from the accounts, and it says so. Never quote the estimate to an owner as the result.
- Never sign off a milestone the data should prove. The product will not let you, and it should not.

## Baseline and verification rules

- The baseline is recorded once, before the saving is measured. From the management accounts,
  enter net sales and loaded labour for the period. From till and payroll, the period must run
  Monday to Sunday, so weekly fixed pay (salaries, guaranteed hours) lines up with the sales it
  is set against.
- A verification is a period signed off from the management accounts: net sales and loaded
  labour. It counts only if it starts after the baseline ends and on or after programme week 1,
  and has sales. A period that overlaps the baseline proves nothing.
- Once a saving is verified, the baseline, the start week and the gain share are fixed. To
  change them, start a new programme.
- The saving is baseline labour % less verified labour %, applied to annualised verified sales.

## Pricing

Two routes, agreed per client before week 1:

- Bundled into the WS fee. The programme raises the value of the monthly service and protects retention.
- Gain share. A percentage of the verified annual saving. The product only ever calculates a fee
  on a verified saving, never on an estimate. Agree the percentage, the baseline period and the
  verification period in the engagement letter, and record them on the programme before week 1.

## Benchmarks

Operators who share their figures see how their sites compare with other WS clients over the last
four finished weeks: labour %, sales per labour hour, hours over demand and forecast error. Only
quartiles leave the cohort. Nothing is shown from fewer than ten sites or three other operators,
or where one operator would make up more than half of the figures, and the asking operator is
always left out. Under UK GDPR these are business figures, not personal data, but client
confidentiality still applies: confirm the sharing clause in the engagement letter before an
operator opts in.
